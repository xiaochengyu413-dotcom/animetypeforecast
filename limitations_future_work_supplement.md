## 5.2.4 Limitations and Future Improvements

Although the preliminary experiments show that the combination of Bayesian weighted ratings and Prophet can capture the long-term evolution of some major anime themes, the current project still has several limitations that need to be addressed before the final thesis is completed.

First, the current theme construction still depends heavily on the original Bangumi tag system, and in some experiments only the first tag is used as the representative theme of a work. This rule is simple to implement, but it causes obvious classification bias and sample loss. Many anime belong to multiple themes at the same time, such as campus + romance + comedy or daily life + healing + school, while the first tag is not always the most semantically important one. As a result, some themes have very short time spans and very few quarterly observations, which weakens the explanatory power of the forecasting results. In the next stage, the project should replace the single-tag rule with a standardized multi-label theme system based on keyword mapping, tag merging, and noise filtering, and then aggregate the data into approximately 10 to 15 stable high-level theme categories.

Second, the present study uses quarterly average weighted rating as the prediction target and treats it as an indicator of popularity. However, rating level and market popularity are related but not identical concepts. A theme may receive high scores from a relatively small core audience, while another theme may have broader exposure, higher discussion volume, and stronger commercial influence but only moderate ratings. Therefore, the current model is better interpreted as forecasting the future reception quality of themes rather than the full concept of popularity. A more complete framework should combine weighted rating with additional indicators such as vote count, number of collected users, discussion activity, and the number of titles released in each quarter, so that a composite popularity index can be constructed.

Third, the data source is currently limited to the Bangumi Archive dataset. This dataset is valuable because it is open, structured, and historically rich, but it still reflects the preferences and tagging habits of a specific user community. It may not fully represent the broader anime market, especially in terms of global audience behavior, commercial performance, or platform-level recommendation effects. In addition, some newly released works may have unstable ratings because the number of users participating in scoring is still growing. In future work, the robustness of the conclusions can be improved by comparing Bangumi-based results with other public platforms or by performing sensitivity analysis on different time windows and sample thresholds.
Under the current archive snapshot dated March 10, 2026, the latest complete quarter in the backtest window (2025Q4) should also be read carefully, because interaction signals such as votes and collections are still accumulating and can make the observed actual values, especially for popularity-oriented indicators, look lower than those of more mature quarters.

Fourth, the present modeling strategy mainly relies on Prophet as a univariate forecasting model. Prophet is suitable for trend and seasonality analysis and performs well when the time series is relatively smooth, but it has limited ability to model abrupt structural changes, interactions among themes, or external shocks such as platform policy changes, highly influential franchises, or unusual release schedules. Moreover, the current report mainly evaluates the model through visual fit and trend interpretation. In the final stage, a more rigorous evaluation should be added through rolling-origin validation or time-based train-test splitting, and Prophet should be compared with baseline methods such as seasonal naive forecasting, ARIMA/SARIMA, or exponential smoothing. Quantitative metrics such as MAE, RMSE, and MAPE should also be reported.

Fifth, the quarterly aggregation strategy improves stability but also introduces information loss. Within a quarter, the number of released works may vary substantially, and averaging all works into one value can hide differences in release volume, rating dispersion, and the effect of exceptionally influential titles. This means that a quarter with only a few anime may be treated the same as a quarter with many anime, even though their statistical reliability is very different. To address this issue, future work should record the number of observations in each quarter, add minimum sample-size thresholds, and consider weighted aggregation or uncertainty adjustment for sparse quarters.

Overall, the current project should be regarded as an exploratory forecasting study with encouraging preliminary results rather than a finalized and fully validated prediction system. The main improvement directions for the remaining work are: refining the theme classification system, broadening the definition of popularity, strengthening model comparison and out-of-sample evaluation, and improving robustness under sparse and imbalanced data conditions. These improvements will make the final conclusions more reliable, interpretable, and academically convincing.
